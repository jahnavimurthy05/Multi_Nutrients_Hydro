Hydroponics


Hydroponics is a modern, soil-less method of growing plants using nutrient-rich water solutions. Instead of relying on traditional soil-based farming, hydroponics provides plants with all essential minerals directly through water, allowing roots to absorb nutrients more efficiently.
This innovative farming technique has gained popularity because it enables faster plant growth, higher yields, and better resource management. Hydroponics uses up to 90% less water, requires less space, and allows cultivation in places where soil quality is poor—such as urban rooftops, indoor farms, or arid regions.
With precise control over pH, temperature, nutrient concentration, and light, hydroponic systems reduce the risk of pests and diseases, minimize environmental impact, and ensure year-round production. As global food demand rises and agricultural land shrinks, hydroponics is emerging as a sustainable and scalable solution for future farming.

Methods To Grow:


1. Kratky Method (No Electricity - Easiest Method)


The Kratky method is the simplest hydroponic technique.It needs no pump, no electricity, no moving parts.
How it works:
Plants grow in cups placed over a container filled with nutrient solution. Roots partly stay in water and partly in air. As water level drops, roots get oxygen naturally.
Best for:
Lettuce, spinach, herbs (basil, mint, coriander), microgreens.
Materials:
  • Plastic container/jar
  • Net cup
  • Nutrient solution
  • Cocopeat or clay pebbles


Deep Water Culture (DWC)


The plant roots float in a nutrient-rich water tank.
How it works:
An air pump provides oxygen to roots. Roots stay submerged in nutrient solution at all times.
Best for:
Lettuce, kale, herbs, strawberries.
Materials:
  • Bucket/container
  • Air pump + air stone
  • Net pots
  • Hydroponic nutrients


3. Nutrient Film Technique (NFT)


A thin film of nutrient water continuously flows over the roots.
How it works:
Plants grow in channels/pipes. A water pump circulates nutrient solution. Roots get both nutrients and oxygen.
Best for:
Leafy greens, herbs.
Materials:
  • PVC pipes
  • Water pump
  • Reservoir tank
  • Net cups


4.Wick System (No Electricity)


One of the easiest systems for beginners.
How it works:
Plant sits in growing medium. A wick pulls nutrient solution from reservoir to roots.
Best for:
Small herbs, leafy greens.
Materials:
  • Container
  • Wick (cotton rope)
  • Nutrient tank
  • Cocopeat/perlites

Know Your Crop:


Tomato

tomatoes grow in nutrient-rich water instead of soil, allowing faster and cleaner growth. They receive precise nutrients, resulting in consistent size, flavor, and higher yield. These tomatoes can be grown year-round indoors, making them ideal for urban and controlled environments.
Lettuce

Hydroponic lettuce grows quickly in water-based systems, using less space and water than traditional farming. It stays crisp and fresh because the nutrients and environment are carefully controlled.
Cucumber

Hydroponic cucumbers grow rapidly in nutrient-rich water, producing clean and uniform fruits. Controlled conditions help reduce pests and diseases, resulting in healthier plants. They yield more in less space, making them ideal for vertical and greenhouse farming.
Basil

Hydroponic basil grows fast and aromatic because it receives precise nutrients and constant moisture. It stays fresh, vibrant, and pest-free due to controlled indoor conditions.
Strawberry

Hydroponic strawberries grow in nutrient-rich water, producing clean and juicy berries without soil. Controlled temperature and light help the plants yield sweeter and more consistent fruit.
Microgreens

They are highly nutritious, offering concentrated vitamins, minerals, and flavor. These tiny greens require very little space, making them ideal for indoor and small-scale farming.
Mint

Hydroponic mint grows vigorously with constant moisture and balanced nutrients, producing lush, aromatic leaves. It stays clean and pest-free because it's grown in a controlled, soil-less environment. This herb can be harvested repeatedly, giving continuous fresh mint year-round.

Mistakes to Avoid

Not checking pH regularly
pH decides how well plants absorb nutrients. If pH goes out of range (5.5-6.5), plants show yellow leaves,
slow growth, and nutrient lockout. Check daily and adjust using pH Up/Down.

poor oxygen supply to roots
Roots need oxygen for nutrient uptake. Without air stones or proper water movement, roots suffocate, rot,
and turn brown. Use strong air pumps to keep water oxygen-rich.

Water temperature too high
Hot water (above 25°C) reduces dissolved oxygen and encourages root rot.
Keep nutrient solution 18 - 22°C by shading the tank or cooling it if needed

Ignoring water level changes
When water gets low, roots dry out at the top and burn due to concentrated nutrients.
Check water every 2 - 3 days and top-up with plain water (not nutrients).
